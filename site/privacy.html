Privacy policy

Last updated 29 September 2026

The short version

  • Mimi is an app that runs on your own computer. There is no Mimi server.
  • The Mimi project collects nothing: no account, no analytics, no telemetry.
  • Your conversations, memories, email, calendars and contacts stay on your computer, in an encrypted database.
  • Mimi contacts other services, such as Google or Telegram, only when you use a feature that needs them, and always straight from your computer.
  • Data from Google is used only to show and manage your calendar for you. It is never sent to the Mimi project.

Who this covers

This policy covers the Mimi app and this website. Mimi is a free, open-source project published on GitHub. "We" means the people who make Mimi.

What we collect

Nothing. Mimi runs entirely on your computer, and we don't operate any server for it. There's no account to create, and the app sends us no analytics, usage statistics, crash reports or telemetry. We never receive your conversations, your memories, your email, your calendars, your contacts or anything else you do in Mimi.

Where your data is kept

Everything Mimi keeps is stored on your computer: your conversations, what it remembers about you, your email, your contacts, your reminders, and the passwords and sign-ins for the accounts you connect. It's kept in an encrypted database. The key to that database is kept in your computer's keychain, or, on a system without one, in a file that only your user account can read.

You can remove any connected account in Mimi's settings, and see, change or delete what it remembers about you. Deleting Mimi's data folder deletes all of it.

When Mimi contacts other services

Mimi connects to another service only when you use a feature that needs it, and always directly from your computer. Nothing passes through us. Each of these services has its own privacy policy.

  • Google, if you connect Google Calendar (by signing in with Google, or with your calendar's private address). See Google user data below.
  • Other calendar and contact services you connect, such as iCloud, Fastmail or Nextcloud.
  • Your email provider, to fetch and send your mail if you connect an email account. To find your provider's servers when you set it up, Mimi may look at your email domain's public settings; it doesn't use a lookup service. Pictures in an email are loaded from the internet only when you click "Load images" on that message.
  • Telegram, if you connect a Telegram bot. Messages between you and your bot go through Telegram's servers and are not end-to-end encrypted, so Telegram can read them.
  • Hugging Face, when you download an AI model for Mimi to run on your computer. Only the model file is requested.
  • A cloud AI service you choose, such as OpenAI, Anthropic or Mistral. Mimi uses models on your computer by default. If you choose a cloud model instead, your messages and the information needed to answer them (for example, what Mimi remembers about the topic, or the calendar events or emails you ask about) are sent to that service. Mimi shows clearly whenever a cloud model is in use.
  • TypeSafe, only if you choose its Jev model to sort your email, with your own key. The emails it sorts are sent to TypeSafe for that.
  • GitHub, only if you turn on "Check for new versions", which is off by default. Mimi then asks GitHub for the latest release about once a day. The request carries no information about you. The install command also downloads Mimi from GitHub.

Google user data

This section explains how Mimi uses information it receives from Google when you choose "Sign in with Google" to connect Google Calendar.

What Mimi asks for, and why

https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar.events

To show the events on your calendars in Mimi, and to add, change or remove events when you ask it to or have given it permission.

https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar.calendarlist.readonly

To list your calendars, with their names and colours and whether you can add events to them.

Mimi asks for nothing else from your Google account.

How it's used

  • Only to show your calendar in Mimi and to let the assistant answer your questions about it and manage events for you.
  • Mimi asks for your approval before it adds, changes or removes an event, unless you've told it in Settings › Permissions that it doesn't need to ask.
  • Mimi never uses it for advertising, never sells it, and never uses it to train AI models.

How it's stored

Mimi talks to Google directly from your computer. Your Google sign-in is stored encrypted in Mimi's database on your device. Mimi reads your events from Google when it needs them rather than keeping a copy, though details you ask about can appear in your conversations and in reminders you set for an event, which stay on your computer too.

Sharing

Your Google data is never sent to the Mimi project, and Mimi doesn't give it to anyone else. It leaves your computer only when you direct it to: if you've chosen a cloud AI model, the event details needed to answer a question you ask go to that service as part of your request; and if you have Mimi send you reminders or summaries through Telegram, those messages go through Telegram.

Removing Mimi's access

  • In Mimi, disconnect Google Calendar in Settings › Connections. This also asks Google to revoke Mimi's access.
  • Or, at any time, in your Google account: go to myaccount.google.com → Security → Third-party access (also called "Your connections to third-party apps and services") and remove Mimi.

Limited Use

Mimi's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

This website

This website has no cookies, no analytics and no scripts from other companies, and it loads nothing from other sites. It is hosted by Vercel, which handles the technical information any website receives, such as your IP address, in order to deliver the pages.

Changes to this policy

If Mimi's handling of your data changes, we'll update this page and the date at the top.

Contact

Questions about this policy or about Mimi's privacy: open an issue on GitHub.
