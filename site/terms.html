The short version

  • Mimi is free, open-source software. The AGPL license is what legally applies to it.
  • It runs on your computer. There's no service or account, so your data and backups are in your hands.
  • It comes with no warranty. The AI can make mistakes, so check anything important.
  • The services you connect to it, like Google or Telegram, have their own terms, and you need to follow them.

The license

Mimi is free, open-source software published under the GNU Affero General Public License, version 3 or later (AGPL). The license decides how you may use, copy, change and share Mimi's code. These terms don't replace it or take anything away from it. They only add a few notes in plain language. Where the two differ, the license wins.

No service, no account

Mimi is an app that runs on your own computer. The project operates no server and offers no online service, and there's no account to create. That also means nobody else looks after your copy of Mimi. Your computer, the data Mimi keeps on it and your backups of that data are your responsibility.

Provided as is

Mimi is provided "as is", without warranty of any kind, and the people who make it are not liable for damage that comes from using it, as far as the law allows. This is the same disclaimer as sections 15 and 16 of the AGPL, which is the version that legally applies.

The AI can be wrong

  • Mimi's answers, summaries, email sorting and drafts come from an AI model, and AI models make mistakes. Check anything that matters, such as dates, times, amounts, names and what an email says, before you rely on it.
  • By default, Mimi asks for your approval before it does anything that sends, changes or deletes something, like sending an email or changing your calendar. Read the details before you approve.
  • In Settings › Permissions you can let it do some of these things without asking. If you do, you're responsible for what it sends and changes on your behalf.

Services you connect

Mimi can connect to services you already use: Google Calendar, your email provider, other calendar and contact services, Telegram, and cloud AI services you choose. Each is run by its own company, under its own terms. When you connect one, you use your own account and, where one is needed, your own API key, and you agree to follow that service's terms. For example, Google's terms apply when you use Google Calendar through Mimi. See the privacy policy for what Mimi exchanges with each of them.

Acceptable use

  • send spam or unwanted bulk messages;
  • get into accounts, mailboxes or calendars that aren't yours, or that you don't have permission to use.
